# Question design — rules and worked examples

Load this when writing or reviewing the questions of a magic questionnaire.

## The axes

Pick 4–6 of these; never two questions on the same axis.

| Axis | Question shape | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Stage / identity** | "Where are you right now?" | Fit fence. Exits out-of-fit people at step 1. |
| **Activity** | "How many X do you do per week?" | Separates the starting from the stuck. Cheap, easy opener. |
| **Problem awareness** | "Can you explain what your work does for the business?" | Reveals the gap the offer closes. Best slot for the education block. |
| **Symptom** | "When did someone last notice your impact?" | The core problem, asked without jargon. |
| **Blocker** | "What actually stops you?" | The emotional / behavioral cause. Options = the real blockers. |
| **Confidence** | "How do you feel about X?" | Gives permission to admit fear; drives tone of the ending. |
| **Aspiration** | "What would change if you got there?" | Optional free text. Highest-signal answer for the human reviewer. |
| **Commitment** | "How much time can you give this per week?" | Only for programs where effort is a precondition. |

## Writing the answers — the pain ladder

Order options from best state to worst. The bottom rung is what makes the
questionnaire honest; it must be phrased so that choosing it feels safe.

Bad (neutral, nobody recognizes themselves):

- Beginner / Intermediate / Advanced / Expert

Good (label = claim, sub-label = lived experience):

- **I can explain it in numbers** — revenue, cost, risk, uptime
- **I know the impact but freeze when explaining it**
- **I know what the system does, not why it matters**
- **I've never thought about it**

Checks for every option:

- Would a real respondent say this sentence about themselves? (If it sounds
  like the business talking, rewrite it.)
- Is the sub-label concrete — a situation, not an adjective?
- Does it map to a different reaction, score, or route than its neighbors?
  If two options lead to identical outcomes and identical reactions, merge
  them.

## Writing the reactions

One reaction per option, 1 sentence, max ~110 characters.

| Answer type | Reaction intent | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Strong | Validate + raise the bar | "Great baseline — now it's about the details that set you apart." |
| Typical | Normalize + promise | "That's the most common pattern, and the most fixable one." |
| Painful | Normalize + reduce shame | "You're far from alone. Naming it is the first step." |
| Out of fit | Kind + honest | "Early is fine — the next screens will point you to the right starting place." |

Never: sarcasm, guilt, scare numbers, "you're doing it wrong".

## Writing the education block

Exactly one. Two panels, two to three sentences each.

- **The problem**: the mechanism behind the answer the respondent just gave.
  Name the cause, not the symptom.
- **The solution**: how the offer addresses that mechanism, without a CTA.

Example for a career program:

> **The problem.** Promotions are rarely decided by who writes the best code.
> They're decided by who can translate delivery into business impact — and
> most strong engineers were never taught to.
>
> **The solution.** We excavate your real deliveries and turn them into
> impact stories your manager, a recruiter or a hiring panel can repeat.

## Worked example A — high-ticket coaching / mentoring program

Decision: book a free intro call, nurture, or decline.
Fit: mid-level+ professionals doing above-title work. Not fit: beginners with
nothing to build on, people wanting a done-for-you service.

1. **Where are you right now?** *(stage — fence)*
   - Just starting out — first job or under 2 years → *exit route*
   - Mid-level, already doing more than my title — delivering, but the title doesn't follow
   - Senior, aiming for the next step — staff, principal, or abroad
   - Leading people or architecture — and want a wider reach
2. **What does your work do for the business?** *(problem awareness — education block here)*
3. **When did someone above you last notice your impact?** *(symptom)*
   - It's recognized and cited
   - Only when something breaks and I fix it
   - My team knows, my manager doesn't
   - Honestly, I don't know if anyone sees it
4. **What actually stops you?** *(blocker)*
   - Fear of losing the stability I built
   - Interviews — especially explaining what I already know
   - I'm the hero who fixes everything and can't delegate
   - I'm exhausted and questioning whether I can do it
5. **What would change if you got there?** *(aspiration — optional free text)*

Endings: book (fit ≥ threshold), guide + newsletter (early), kind decline with
one pointer (exit route).

## Worked example B — self-serve SaaS onboarding

Decision: which first screen / template / plan to show.

1. **What brings you here today?** *(goal)* — 4 jobs-to-be-done.
2. **How big is your team?** *(segment, 2×2 grid)* — Just me / 2–10 / 11–50 / 50+.
3. **What are you using now?** *(switching context)* — Spreadsheets / Competitor-class tool / Paper / Nothing yet.
4. **What would make this a win in 30 days?** *(aspiration, 2×2 grid)*

No not-a-fit ending here; routes are onboarding paths. Make Q2–Q4 skippable
after Q1 for high-volume products.

## Worked example C — lead-magnet assessment

Decision: show a profile label for free, gate the full report behind email.

- 6–8 Likert-style questions on one theme, each still a pain ladder with
  label + sub-label.
- Score into 3–4 named profiles ("The firefighter", "The invisible senior"…).
- Free: the profile name + 2-sentence read-back. Behind email: the full
  prescription.

## Review checklist for a question set

- [ ] Every question maps to one axis, and no axis is repeated
- [ ] Q1 is answerable in two seconds and works as a fit fence
- [ ] Every option has label + concrete sub-label + reaction
- [ ] Every question has a one-line "why we ask"
- [ ] The bottom rung of each ladder is safe to choose
- [ ] Exactly one education block, on the problem-awareness question
- [ ] No contact data before the final step
- [ ] Nothing copied from the reference that inspired it
